## Common setting for AX-01 and AX-02

Both entries specialize `[fontana2022term]` Section 3 (arXiv:2202.00929v4, pp. 6–10) to the setting of `RESEARCH_PROPOSAL.md` Section 3. The specialization is the one the source itself exercises in its Example 3.10 (pp. 10–11): "We consider only the presence of the expected jump dates S, assuming T = ∅ for simplicity of presentation, so that S^0 = exp(∫_0^· r_u du)."

Source objects (their notation, pp. 4–7):
- A stochastic basis (Ω, F, F, Q), F = (F_t)_{t≥0} satisfying the usual conditions, carrying a d-dimensional Brownian motion W and an integer-valued random measure μ(dt,dx) on R_+ × E with compensator ν(dt,dx) = λ_t(dx) dt (p. 6).
- Two finite sets of dates: roll-over dates T = {t_1, t_2, …} (atoms of the measure η(du) = du + Σ_n δ_{t_n}(du) of (2.3), p. 4, at which the numéraire S^0_t = exp(∫_0^t ρ_u η(du)) of (2.2) jumps) and expected jump dates S = {s_1, …, s_M} (p. 6). ρ is the risk-free rate process.
- Bond prices P(t,T) = exp(−∫_{(t,T]} f(t,u) η(du)), eq. (3.1), p. 6.
- Forward rates, eq. (3.2), p. 6: f(t,T) = f(0,T) + ∫_0^t α(s,T) ds + ∫_0^t φ(s,T) dW_s + ∫_0^t ∫_E ψ(s,x,T) μ̃(ds,dx) + V(t,T), where V(·,T) is a pure jump adapted process with {ΔV(·,T) ≠ 0} ⊆ Ω × S.
- Integrated coefficients (p. 7): ᾱ(t,T) = ∫_{[t,T]} α(t,u) η(du), φ̄(t,T) = ∫_{[t,T]} φ(t,u) η(du), ψ̄(t,x,T) = ∫_{[t,T]} ψ(t,x,u) η(du), V̄(t,T) = ∫_{[t,T]} ΔV(t,u) η(du).
- Q is a risk-neutral measure if P(·,T)/S^0 is a local martingale under Q for every T > 0 (p. 7).

Source standing assumption, Assumption 3.3 (p. 7), all five parts: (i) T ↦ f(0,T) is (F_0 ⊗ B(R_+))-measurable, real-valued, with ∫_0^T |f(0,u)| η(du) < ∞ for all T > 0; (ii) α : Ω × R_+^2 → R is progressively measurable, α(t,T) = 0 for 0 ≤ T < t, and ∫_0^T ∫_0^u |α(s,u)| ds η(du) < ∞ for all T > 0; (iii) φ : Ω × R_+^2 → R^d is progressively measurable, φ(t,T) = 0 for 0 ≤ T < t, and Σ_{i=1}^d ∫_0^T (∫_0^u |φ_i(s,u)|² ds)^{1/2} η(du) < ∞ for all T > 0; (iv) ψ is (P ⊗ B(E) ⊗ B(R_+))-measurable, ψ(t,x,T) = 0 for 0 ≤ T < t, and ∫_0^T ∫_E ∫_0^T |ψ(s,x,u)|² η(du) λ_s(dx) ds < ∞; (v) ∫_0^T |ΔV(s,u)| η(du) < ∞ for all s ∈ S and ΔV(t,T) = 0 for all 0 ≤ T < t.

Source result, Theorem 3.5 (p. 8). Under Assumption 3.3, Q is a risk-neutral measure if and only if the integrability condition (3.5) holds, the random variable (3.6) e^{−∫_{(τ,T]} ΔV(τ,u) η(du) δ_S(τ) − Δρ_τ δ_T(τ)} is sigma-integrable with respect to F_{τ−} for every τ ∈ S ∪ T, and: (i) f(t,t) = ρ_t (Q ⊗ dt)-a.e.; (ii) on [0,T], (Q ⊗ dt)-a.e., ᾱ(t,T) = ½ ‖φ̄(t,T)‖² + ∫_E (e^{−ψ̄(t,x,T)} − 1 + ψ̄(t,x,T)) λ_t(dx); (iii) for every j, a.s., f(t_j−,t_j) = ρ_{t_j−} − log E[e^{−Δρ_{t_j}} | F_{t_j−}]; (iv) for every i = 1, …, M, a.s., E[ e^{−Δρ_{s_i} δ_T(s_i)} ( e^{−∫_{(s_i,T]} ΔV(s_i,u) η(du)} − 1 ) | F_{s_i−} ] = 0.

Our specialization, applied identically in both entries (each item is a simplification we impose, repeated field by field below):
- (S1) No roll-over dates: T = ∅, so η is Lebesgue measure du, the numéraire is B_t = exp(∫_0^t r_u du), the bond price is P(t,T) = exp(−∫_t^T f(t,u) du), and ∫_{[t,T]} · η(du) = ∫_t^T · du. Assumption 3.6 (p. 9: "the set {(ω,t) ∈ Ω × T : Δρ_t(ω) ≠ 0} is evanescent and S ∩ T = ∅") holds trivially since Ω × T is empty; condition (iii) of Theorem 3.5 is vacuous (no j); the factor e^{−Δρ_{s_i} δ_T(s_i)} in condition (iv) equals 1 (p. 10, line after Assumption 3.6: "under Assumption 3.6 the term Δρ_{s_i} δ_T(s_i) in condition (iv) vanishes").
- (S2) No random measure: ψ ≡ 0 and μ absent. Condition (3.5) and the ∫_E term in condition (ii) vanish; Assumption 3.3(iv) is void.
- (S3) Overnight rate defined as the short end: ρ_t := f(t,t) for all t ≥ 0, which is (3.12) in Remark 3.9 (p. 10). Condition (i) of Theorem 3.5 holds by definition. In our notation r_t = f(t,t).
- (S4) Names: our α is their α; our σ (R^d-valued) is their φ; our W, d, Q, F_t are theirs; our scheduled dates T_1 < … < T_N are their s_1 < … < s_M with N = M; our ξ_n(u) is their ΔV(T_n, u), so their V(t,T) is our Σ_{n : T_n ≤ t} ξ_n(T); our r is their ρ; our B is their S^0. T_0 = 0 is an interval endpoint only, not a jump date (there is no F_{0−}); ξ_0 ≡ 0.
- (S5) S is a finite set of fixed dates, as in the source (Remark 3.1, p. 6, notes that countably many predictable times would also work; we do not use that).
- (S6) Direction of use: Theorem 3.5 is an equivalence. We never use its "if" direction and we never mention bond prices, the numéraire, or local martingales: the axioms are conditions (ii) and (iv) themselves, imposed as hypotheses, so that Lean sees only integral identities and one conditional-expectation identity.
- (S7) Standing hypotheses carried into Lean as fields of the same structure (our notation): f(0,·) is B(R_+)-measurable and locally integrable (Assumption 3.3(i)); α is progressively measurable, α(t,T) = 0 for T < t, and ∫_0^T ∫_0^u |α(s,u)| ds du < ∞ a.s. for all T (3.3(ii)); σ is progressively measurable, σ(t,T) = 0 for T < t, and Σ_i ∫_0^T (∫_0^u |σ_i(s,u)|² ds)^{1/2} du < ∞ a.s. for all T (3.3(iii)); for each n, ξ_n(·) is F_{T_n} ⊗ B(R_+)-measurable, ξ_n(u) = 0 for u < T_n, and ∫_0^T |ξ_n(u)| du < ∞ for all T (3.3(v)). The source states the integrability in 3.3(ii)–(v) without an explicit "a.s."; we impose it a.s.

Lean file: `lean/Upstream/HJMScheduled.lean` (to be written by Lean), one structure `Upstream.HJMScheduled` whose data fields are the filtered probability space, `d`, `N`, the dates `τ : ℕ → ℝ` with `τ 0 = 0` and strictly increasing on `Set.Iic N` (the encoding Claim 002 already uses), `f₀ : ℝ → ℝ`, `α : ℝ → ℝ → Ω → ℝ`, `σ : ℝ → ℝ → Ω → EuclideanSpace ℝ (Fin d)`, `ξ : ℕ → ℝ → Ω → ℝ`; whose hypothesis fields for (S7) are `f₀_measurable`, `f₀_locallyIntegrable`, `α_progMeasurable`, `α_zero_of_lt`, `α_integrable`, `σ_progMeasurable`, `σ_zero_of_lt`, `σ_integrable`, `ξ_measurable`, `ξ_zero_of_lt`, `ξ_integrable`; and whose axiom fields are `drift_integrated` (AX-01) and `jump_martingale` (AX-02). The Brownian motion W and the forward-rate equation (3.2) itself are not fields: no claim on the roadmap uses the dynamics of f beyond the two conditions, and adding them would only enlarge the interface (`RESEARCH_PROPOSAL.md` Section 7, axiom interface risk). If a later claim needs W or (3.2), a new ledger entry adds them.

### AX-01  HJM drift condition between meetings (integrated form)
Source: Claudio Fontana, Zorana Grbac, Thorsten Schmidt, Term structure modelling with overnight rates beyond stochastic continuity, 2022, `[fontana2022term]` (arXiv:2202.00929v4), Theorem 3.5 condition (ii), p. 8; integrated coefficients ᾱ, φ̄ defined p. 7; original drift condition `[heath1992bond]`; the source itself notes (p. 9) that without stochastic discontinuities the characterization reduces to (3.5) with conditions (i)–(ii).
Statement (paper notation): Under Assumption 3.3, for every T > 0, on [0,T] it holds (Q ⊗ dt)-a.e. that
  ᾱ(t,T) = ½ ‖φ̄(t,T)‖² + ∫_E ( e^{−ψ̄(t,x,T)} − 1 + ψ̄(t,x,T) ) λ_t(dx),
where ᾱ(t,T) = ∫_{[t,T]} α(t,u) η(du) and φ̄(t,T) = ∫_{[t,T]} φ(t,u) η(du) ∈ R^d, ‖·‖ the Euclidean norm.
Statement (our notation): For every T > 0, for (Q ⊗ dt)-a.e. (ω, t) ∈ Ω × [0,T],
  (AX-01)  ∫_t^T α(t,u) du = ½ | ∫_t^T σ(t,u) du |²,
with |·| the Euclidean norm on R^d and both integrals Lebesgue integrals in u (the R^d-valued one componentwise). Equivalently, since α(t,u) = σ(t,u) = 0 for u < t, the identity may be read with ∫_0^T in place of ∫_t^T.
  Derived form, not part of the axiom: `RESEARCH_PROPOSAL.md` Section 3 states A1 pointwise, α(t,T) = σ(t,T) · ∫_t^T σ(t,u) du. That is the T-derivative of (AX-01); the two agree dT-a.e. for each fixed (ω,t) by Lebesgue differentiation of both sides (each side is absolutely continuous in T under (S7)), and exactly, for every T, when u ↦ α(t,u) and u ↦ σ(t,u) are piecewise constant with the same finite partition (the setting of Claim 002). Any claim that uses the pointwise form derives it from (AX-01) as a step with its own justification; nothing here proves the pointwise form.
Assumptions in the source: Assumption 3.3 in full (all five parts, quoted above); the theorem is stated for a risk-neutral Q; condition (ii) is asserted for each fixed T > 0 as an identity (Q ⊗ dt)-a.e. on [0,T]; the integrals ᾱ, φ̄ are η-integrals over the closed interval [t,T]; ‖·‖ is the Euclidean norm on R^d.
Simplifications we impose: (S1) T = ∅ and η = du, so ᾱ(t,T) = ∫_t^T α(t,u) du and φ̄(t,T) = ∫_t^T σ(t,u) du and [t,T] versus (t,T] is immaterial; (S2) ψ ≡ 0, so the ∫_E term is dropped and condition (3.5) is void; (S3) r_t := f(t,t), which makes condition (i) automatic and is not otherwise used here; (S4) renaming φ → σ, s_n → T_n; (S6) the condition is taken as a hypothesis, not derived from the local martingale property; (S7) Assumption 3.3(i)–(iii) and (v) become fields, with the integrability imposed a.s. The quantifier structure is unchanged: for every T, a.e. in (ω,t).
Lean: structure `Upstream.HJMScheduled`, field `drift_integrated : ∀ T, 0 < T → ∀ᵐ (p : Ω × ℝ) ∂(μ.prod (volume.restrict (Set.Icc 0 T))), ∫ u in p.2..T, α p.2 u p.1 = (1/2) * ‖∫ u in p.2..T, σ p.2 u p.1‖^2` (informal; Lean fixes the exact spelling of the product measure and of the interval integral).

### AX-02  Jump martingale condition at scheduled dates
Source: Claudio Fontana, Zorana Grbac, Thorsten Schmidt, Term structure modelling with overnight rates beyond stochastic continuity, 2022, `[fontana2022term]` (arXiv:2202.00929v4), Theorem 3.5 condition (iv), p. 8, together with the sigma-integrability of (3.6), p. 8; simplified by Assumption 3.6, pp. 9–10; the form we use is the displayed equation of Remark 3.9, p. 10, under (3.12); exercised with T = ∅ in Example 3.10, pp. 10–11, whose compensator U^d in (3.14) is deduced "from condition (iv) of Theorem 3.5".
Statement (paper notation): Under Assumption 3.3, for every T > 0 and every i = 1, …, M, the random variable e^{−∫_{(s_i,T]} ΔV(s_i,u) η(du) δ_S(s_i) − Δρ_{s_i} δ_T(s_i)} is sigma-integrable with respect to F_{s_i−} and, a.s.,
  E[ e^{−Δρ_{s_i} δ_T(s_i)} ( e^{−∫_{(s_i,T]} ΔV(s_i,u) η(du)} − 1 ) | F_{s_i−} ] = 0.
Under Assumption 3.6 the factor e^{−Δρ_{s_i} δ_T(s_i)} vanishes from the condition (p. 10). Under (3.12), ρ_t := f(t,t), Remark 3.9 (p. 10) writes conditions (iii)–(iv) as
  E[ e^{−∫_{[s_i,T]} ΔV(s_i,u) η(du)} | F_{s_i−} ] = 1,  for all i = 1, …, M.
Statement (our notation): For every n = 1, …, N and every real T ≥ T_n, Q-a.s.,
  (AX-02)  E[ exp( −∫_{T_n}^T ξ_n(u) du ) | F_{T_n−} ] = 1,
where F_{T_n−} = σ(∪_{t < T_n} F_t), the integrand is the F_{T_n} ⊗ B(R_+)-measurable jump ξ_n(·) of the curve at T_n, and the conditional expectation of the nonnegative random variable exp(−∫_{T_n}^T ξ_n(u) du) is taken in [0, ∞]; the identity forces that random variable to be integrable (its expectation is 1), which is the specialization of the source's sigma-integrability requirement. For T < T_n the condition is empty (ξ_n(u) = 0 for u < T_n by (S7), so the integral over (T_n, T] is not formed). This is `RESEARCH_PROPOSAL.md` Section 3, Axiom A2, verbatim.
Assumptions in the source: Assumption 3.3 in full, in particular (v): ∫_0^T |ΔV(s,u)| η(du) < ∞ for all s ∈ S and ΔV(t,T) = 0 for 0 ≤ T < t; V(·,T) adapted and pure jump with jumps only on Ω × S, so ΔV(s_i, ·) is F_{s_i}-measurable; S a finite set of fixed dates; the theorem is stated for a risk-neutral Q and condition (iv) is asserted for every T > 0 (the theorem quantifies over all maturities T > 0) and every i; the integral in (iv) is over the half-open interval (s_i, T] against η, and in Remark 3.9 over the closed interval [s_i, T], which differ only by the η-atom at s_i when s_i ∈ T; Assumption 3.6 (ρ does not jump on T, and S ∩ T = ∅) is needed for the source's own simplification of (iv); (3.12) ρ_t := f(t,t) is an additional definition made in Remark 3.9, not part of Theorem 3.5.
Simplifications we impose: (S1) T = ∅ and η = du: Assumption 3.6 holds vacuously, δ_T(s_i) = 0 so the factor e^{−Δρ_{s_i} δ_T(s_i)} is 1, δ_S(s_i) = 1, (s_i, T] versus [s_i, T] is immaterial, and condition (iii) disappears; (S2) no random measure (irrelevant to this condition beyond the standing assumptions); (S3) r_t := f(t,t) as in (3.12), which is what lets Remark 3.9 rewrite (iv) as a conditional expectation equal to 1 and makes condition (i) automatic; (S4) ξ_n(u) := ΔV(T_n, u), s_i → T_n, M → N, and T_0 = 0 is not a jump date; (S5) finitely many fixed dates; (S6) taken as a hypothesis; (S7) Assumption 3.3(v) becomes the fields `ξ_measurable`, `ξ_zero_of_lt`, `ξ_integrable`. The "sigma-integrable with respect to F_{s_i−}" requirement is replaced by the (stronger, simpler) consequence that E[exp(−∫_{T_n}^T ξ_n(u) du)] = 1 < ∞, which follows from (AX-02) by taking expectations; we do not need sigma-integrability in any weaker form.
Lean: structure `Upstream.HJMScheduled`, field `jump_martingale : ∀ n, 1 ≤ n → n ≤ N → ∀ T, τ n ≤ T → μ[fun ω => Real.exp (-(∫ u in τ n..T, ξ n u ω)) | ℱ_(τ n)⁻] =ᵐ[μ] 1`, where `ℱ_(τ n)⁻` is the sigma-algebra generated by `⋃ t < τ n, ℱ t` (informal; Lean chooses how to spell the left limit of the filtration and Mathlib's conditional expectation `MeasureTheory.condExp`).

## Common setting for AX-03 to AX-08 (standard stochastic calculus for the Claim 015 Upstream route)

These entries implement the human policy of 2026-09-22 (`board/NOTICES.md`): the standard stochastic-calculus inputs that Claim 015's remaining Lean bridge needs are cited here, represented as fields of one hypothesis structure in `lean/Upstream/`, and consumed by `lean/Novel/`. AX-03 to AX-06 cite `[bauerschmidt2020stochastic]` (Cambridge lecture notes, Lent 2020; the registered text `refs/text/bauerschmidt2020stochastic.md` is a tesseract OCR of handwritten notes whose prose is reliable and whose formulas are not; page numbers are the notes' own, printed at the end of each page in the OCR, and OCR line numbers are given so the Auditor can find each passage). Source objects and their notation (`[bauerschmidt2020stochastic]`):
- (C1) From §2.2 on, a filtered probability space (Ω, F, (F_t)_{t≥0}, P) satisfying the usual conditions: F_0 contains all P-null sets and (F_t) is right-continuous (p. 16, OCR lines 549–553).
- (C2) Predictable processes: the predictable σ-algebra on Ω × [0,∞) is generated by the simple processes H = Σ_i H_{i−1} 1_{(t_{i−1},t_i]} with H_{i−1} bounded F_{t_{i−1}}-measurable; adapted left-continuous processes are predictable (p. 14, lines 480–497). Lean's counterpart is Mathlib's `Filtration.predictable` and `IsStronglyPredictable`.
- (C3) Stopping times and the stopped process X^T_t := X_{t∧T}; the optional stopping theorem is quoted in AX-04.
- (C4) Local martingales: a càdlàg adapted X with stopping times T_n ↑ ∞ a.s. ("reducing sequence") such that each X^{T_n} is a martingale (p. 17, lines 573–580); bounded local martingales are martingales (p. 19, lines 637–639).
- (C5) H² is the space of càdlàg martingales bounded in L², with norm ‖X‖_{H²} = (sup_t E X_t²)^{1/2} = (E X_∞²)^{1/2}; H²_c its continuous members (p. 22, lines 726–732). H² is a Hilbert space and H²_c is closed (p. 23).
- (C6) Quadratic variation ⟨M⟩ of a continuous local martingale M: the unique (up to indistinguishability) continuous increasing process with ⟨M⟩_0 = 0 such that M² − ⟨M⟩ is a continuous local martingale, and the UCP limit of the sums Σ (M_{t_i∧t} − M_{t_{i−1}∧t})² along partitions of mesh → 0 (Thm p. 25, lines 796–806). Covariation ⟨M,N⟩ := ¼(⟨M+N⟩ − ⟨M−N⟩) (p. 32, lines 1040–1043) with the properties (p. 33, lines 1048–1062): (i) ⟨M,N⟩ is the unique finite-variation process with MN − ⟨M,N⟩ a continuous local martingale; (ii) bilinear and symmetric; (iii) UCP limit of Σ (M_{t_i}−M_{t_{i−1}})(N_{t_i}−N_{t_{i−1}}); (iv) ⟨M^T,N⟩ = ⟨M^T,N^T⟩ = ⟨M,N⟩_{·∧T} for every stopping time T; (v) for M, N ∈ H²_c, MN − ⟨M,N⟩ is a uniformly integrable martingale and E[(M−M_0)_∞(N−N_0)_∞] = E⟨M,N⟩_∞. Also (p. 31, lines 998–1002): a continuous local martingale M with M_0 = 0 lies in H² iff E⟨M⟩_∞ < ∞, and then M² − ⟨M⟩ is a UI martingale and ‖M‖²_{H²} = E⟨M⟩_∞. The lab writes [M,N] for the source's ⟨M,N⟩ (`ledger/TERMS.md`).
- (C7) A continuous semimartingale is X = X_0 + M + A with M a continuous local martingale, A a continuous finite-variation process, M_0 = A_0 = 0; the decomposition is unique; ⟨X,X'⟩ := ⟨M,M'⟩ (p. 36, lines 1132–1141).
- (C8) The Itô integral H·M = ∫_0^· H_s dM_s, defined in three stages quoted in AX-03 and AX-04: for M ∈ H²_c and simple H (p. 37); for M ∈ H²_c and H ∈ L²(M), the predictable H with ‖H‖_{L²(M)} := (E ∫_0^∞ H_s² d⟨M⟩_s)^{1/2} < ∞ (pp. 39–41); for M a continuous local martingale and H ∈ L²_loc(M), the predictable H with ∫_0^t H_s² d⟨M⟩_s < ∞ for all t ≥ 0 a.s. (pp. 44–46); and for a continuous semimartingale X = X_0 + M + A and H predictable locally bounded, H·X := H·M + H·A with H·A the Lebesgue–Stieltjes integral (p. 47, lines 1457–1461).
- (C9) SDEs (§5.1, pp. 65–67, lines 1952–1991): for locally bounded b : ℝ_+ × ℝ^d → ℝ^d and σ : ℝ_+ × ℝ^d → ℝ^{d×m}, a weak solution of E(σ,b), dX_t = b(t,X_t) dt + σ(t,X_t) dB_t, consists of a filtered probability space satisfying the usual conditions, an m-dimensional (F_t)-Brownian motion B and an F-adapted continuous ℝ^d-valued X with X_t = X_0 + ∫_0^t σ(s,X_s) dB_s + ∫_0^t b(s,X_s) ds. A strong solution is one on a given space with a given B, adapted to the completed filtration generated by B. Pathwise uniqueness (strong uniqueness) means: for fixed (Ω, F, (F_t), P) and B, all solutions of E(σ,b) with the same initial value are indistinguishable.

Our specialization, applied in every entry below (each item is a restriction we impose on the source's generality; the source's theorems are not changed):
- (U1) The stochastic basis is Claim 013's (Ω, F, (F_t)_{t≥0}, Q), which satisfies the usual conditions (C1); time is t ∈ ℝ_{≥0}. We write Q for the source's P.
- (U2) Drivers. Instead of arbitrary continuous local martingales M we integrate only against finitely many fixed driver processes B^1, …, B^m, each a continuous martingale with B^k_0 = 0 and B^k_t ∈ L²(Q) for every t, whose covariations have a deterministic bounded measurable density: [B^k,B^l]_t = ∫_0^t c_{kl}(s) ds. By (C6)(i) and (C5)–(C6)(v) this is equivalent, for such martingales, to B^kB^l − ∫_0^· c_{kl}(s) ds being a martingale on every finite horizon, which is how Lean states it. In Claim 013 the drivers are the 2d Brownian motions W_1, …, W_d, U_1, …, U_d of (13.1) with c the block matrix c_{W_iW_j} = c_{U_iU_j} = δ_{ij}, c_{W_iU_j} = δ_{ij}ρ_j(s), |ρ_j| ≤ 1; the structure does not itself assert that the drivers are Brownian (AX-06 and AX-07 add that hypothesis where their sources need it).
- (U3) Since c is bounded on compacts and d[B^k]_t = c_{kk}(t) dt, a predictable H with ∫_0^t H_s² ds < ∞ a.s. for all t lies in the source's L²_loc(B^k), and one with E ∫_0^T H_s² ds < ∞ lies in L²(B^k 1_{[0,T]}). We use only these two classes, called (U4) and (U5):

### AX-09  Predictability of continuous adapted real-valued processes
Source: [bauerschmidt2020stochastic], Stochastic Calculus and Applications (Lent 2020), p. 13 (definition of the predictable sigma-algebra) and p. 14 (Fact and proof: adapted left-continuous processes are predictable), registered text lines 467–493. This is the source fact already recorded as (C2) above, now given its own field for Claim 023's remaining predictability premise.
Statement (paper notation): For a real-valued adapted process H with left-continuous paths, H is measurable for the predictable sigma-algebra. The source proves this by approximation with adapted simple processes and pointwise limits.
Statement (our notation): For every real-valued process X indexed by nonnegative real time, if X_t is F_t-measurable for every t and every path t ↦ X_t(ω) is continuous, then X is strongly measurable for Mathlib's `Filtration.predictable`, i.e. `IsStronglyPredictable ℱ X`.
Assumptions in the source: A filtration, adaptedness and left-continuous paths. We use that explicit convention; adaptedness supplies the initial-section measurability, and the positive-time assertion is unchanged. The source includes continuous Brownian motion as a predictable example immediately after the fact.
Simplifications we impose: Only real-valued processes with continuous paths everywhere, a subclass of the source's left-continuous processes. Source: [filipovic2000exponential], Theorem 3.2 (p. 6) and Corollaries 3.4–3.5 (p. 7), with the definitions preceding them (the family (5) and BEP(K, n), Definition 3.1; the equivalence ∼ and p_[i], (6)–(8); the Itô process Z, (9); the sets A_i, B, C, D and D′ and the debut τ′, p. 6); proofs in §5 (Lemma 5.1, Corollary 5.2) and §6. Full text is registered in `refs/text/filipovic2000exponential.md`.
Statement (paper notation): "Theorem 3.2. Let K ∈ N, n = (n_1,…,n_K) ∈ N_0^K and Z as above. If Z is consistent with BEP(K,n), then necessarily for 1 ≤ i ≤ K: a_{i,n_i+1;i,n_i+1} = 0 on {p_i(Z) ≠ 0}, dt⊗dP-a.s. (12); b_{i,n_i+1} = 0 on {p_i(Z) ≠ 0} ∩ {p_[i](Z) ≠ 0}, dt⊗dP-a.s. (13). Consequently, Z^{i,n_i+1} is constant on intervals where p_i(Z) ≠ 0 and p_[i](Z) ≠ 0." The second part: for a stopping time τ with [τ] ⊂ D′, with τ′ the debut of (B ∪ C) ∩ [τ,∞[, τ < τ′ on {τ < ∞}, and Z^{i,μ}_{τ+t} = Z^{i,μ}_τ e^{−Z^{i,n_i+1}_τ t} + Z^{i,μ+1}_τ t e^{−Z^{i,n_i+1}_τ t} and Z^{i,n_i}_{τ+t} = Z^{i,n_i}_τ e^{−Z^{i,n_i+1}_τ t} on [0, τ′ − τ[ for 0 ≤ μ ≤ n_i − 1, up to evanescence. With D in place of D′ and τ′ the debut of (∪A_i ∪ B ∪ C) ∩ [τ,∞[, τ′ = ∞ and in addition Z^{i,n_i+1}_{τ+t} = Z^{i,n_i+1}_τ, P-a.s. on {τ < ∞}. Corollary 3.4: if {p_i(Z) = 0} and {p_[i](Z) = 0} have dt⊗dP-measure zero, then Z^{i,n_i+1} is indistinguishable from Z^{i,n_i+1}_0. Corollary 3.5: if p_i(Z_0) ≠ 0 for all i, and no i ≠ j has Z^{i,n_i+1}_0 = Z^{j,n_j+1}_0 or 2Z^{i,n_i+1}_0 = Z^{j,n_j+1}_0, P-a.s., then Z and F(x,Z) are quasi-deterministic, and the exponents are indistinguishable from their initial values. Consistency (Definition 2.1) means the support of Z lies in 𝒵 = {z : sup_x |F(x,z)| < ∞} and discounted bond prices are martingales. Proposition 2.2 derives from it the equation (3): ∂_x F(x,Z) = Σ_i b^i ∂_{z_i} F(x,Z) + ½ Σ_{i,j} a^{ij} (∂²_{z_i z_j} F − ∂_{z_i} F ∫_0^x ∂_{z_j} F dη − ∂_{z_j} F ∫_0^x ∂_{z_i} F dη), for all x ≥ 0, dt⊗dP-a.s.
